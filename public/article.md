# The Impact of Pringle Maneuver on Acid-Base Homeostasis During Hepatic Resection: A Review of Current Evidence

## Abstract

The Pringle maneuver, a temporary occlusion of the hepatic pedicle during liver surgery, is a widely used technique for reducing blood loss during hepatic resection. While its effectiveness in hemorrhage control is well-documented, its potential effects on acid-base balance warrant careful examination. This review analyzes current evidence regarding the relationship between the Pringle maneuver and acid-base homeostasis.

## Introduction

First described by J. Hogarth Pringle in 1908[1], the Pringle maneuver has become a standard technique in liver surgery. The procedure involves temporary occlusion of the hepatic pedicle (portal vein and hepatic artery) to control bleeding during hepatic procedures. Recent studies have suggested potential metabolic consequences of this temporary ischemia[2].

## Current Evidence on Physiological Effects

### 1. Impact on Lactate Metabolism

Research has demonstrated that the liver plays a central role in lactate clearance[3]. During the Pringle maneuver:

- Hepatic blood flow interruption affects lactate clearance[4]
- Studies have shown increased lactate levels during the clamping period[5]
- The degree of lactate elevation correlates with occlusion duration[6]

### 2. Ischemia-Reperfusion Effects

Clinical studies have documented several effects during and after hepatic pedicle clamping[7]:

- Temporary hepatic ischemia occurs during clamping
- Cellular metabolic changes have been observed
- Reperfusion leads to the release of accumulated metabolites
- pH changes have been documented in clinical settings

### 3. Systemic Effects

Multiple studies have investigated the broader physiological impact[8,9]:

- Changes in acid-base parameters during the procedure
- Potential compensatory mechanisms
- Variation in individual patient responses

## Evidence-Based Clinical Considerations

### 1. Timing and Duration

Recent meta-analyses suggest[10]:
- Intermittent clamping shows different metabolic effects compared to continuous clamping
- Optimal duration remains under investigation
- Individual patient factors influence tolerance

## Evidence-Based Clinical Considerations

### 2. Risk Stratification

Studies have identified several factors that may affect outcomes:
- Pre-existing liver function status
- Overall health status
- Surgical complexity
- Duration of procedure

### 3. Monitoring Recommendations

Current clinical guidelines suggest[7]:
- Regular acid-base status assessment
- Lactate level monitoring
- Hemodynamic parameter tracking
- Individualized approach based on patient factors

## Management Strategies Based on Current Evidence

### 1. Surgical Considerations
- Selective use of the Pringle maneuver
- Consideration of alternative techniques when appropriate
- Individualized approach to clamping duration

### 2. Perioperative Management
- Evidence-based monitoring protocols
- Early recognition of metabolic changes
- Appropriate intervention timing

## Discussion

The relationship between the Pringle maneuver and acid-base balance involves multiple physiological pathways that are still being fully elucidated. Current research suggests:
- Complex metabolic responses
- Individual variation in response
- Need for personalized approaches

## Conclusion

While the Pringle maneuver remains an important surgical technique, its effects on acid-base balance deserve careful consideration. More research is needed to fully understand the mechanisms and optimize management strategies.
